Permissions and Security

What the app asks for, and why

Confluence shows this list when an administrator installs the app. Every entry maps to an endpoint the app actually calls. Nothing is requested "just in case".

PermissionUsed for
Read space detailsThe inventory: which spaces exist, their keys, names and homepages. Also reads the blueprint reference and review date the app stores on a space.
Write space detailsCreating the requested space, and writing the blueprint reference and review date onto it.
Read space permissionsReading the site's space-role catalogue, and reading a space's role assignments so drift can be detected.
Write space permissionsAssigning the owner their role on the space the app just created. Nothing else.
Read page detailsPage counts and last-activity dates, for blueprint fidelity reports and the inventory.
Read configurationA single call that reports whether the site uses role-based space access, so the app picks the right creation path.
Write contentStarting Confluence's own page-hierarchy copy task.
Write space (classic)Applying space labels, and creating a space on sites without role-based access.
Read space summary (classic)Polling the status of the copy task, and receiving space events.
Read content summary (classic)Receiving page and label events.
App storageThe app's own data: blueprints, policies, requests, inventory, audit trail.
Report personal dataTelling Atlassian which account ids the app stores, and erasing them on request.

Two scopes are "classic" rather than granular because Confluence offers no granular equivalent for them: the page-hierarchy copy task, its status endpoint, space labels and the space event stream are v1-only surfaces.

What the app does not ask for

  • Reading users. The app has no permission to read user profiles. It stores account ids and resolves

display names only for on-screen rendering.

  • Reading attachments. Attachments are copied by Confluence's task; the app never lists or reads them.
  • Changing site configuration. The app cannot create or edit space roles, cannot change site settings

and cannot run Atlassian's permission-model migration.

  • Anything outside Confluence. No Jira scopes, no organisation scopes.

Who can do what

ActionRequired
Request a spaceAny licensed Confluence user (or a member of the groups an administrator allowed)
See your own requestsThe requester
Browse the blueprint galleryAny eligible requester; a blueprint appears only if you can view its source space
Approve or reject a requestConfluence product administrator
Create, edit or archive blueprintsConfluence product administrator
Edit naming policies, settings, auto-approval rulesConfluence product administrator
View the inventory, drift and audit logConfluence product administrator
Delete all app dataConfluence product administrator, behind a confirmation

Administrator rights are checked in the app's backend on every administrative call, not only by hiding buttons in the interface. A non-administrator who reaches an admin URL directly is refused by the server.

How the app acts in Confluence

The app performs provisioning under its own app identity, not by impersonating a person. It never acts as one of your users. The audit trail records which person requested the space and which person approved it, so attribution is preserved without the app borrowing anyone's account.

This was a deliberate design decision, taken after verifying on a live site that the app can create spaces as itself. The alternative — impersonating the approving administrator — would have given the app the ability to act as a named person, which is a much larger surface for no functional gain.

Safety rules the app follows

  • It never deletes or archives a space or a page, even to clean up after a failed provisioning run.
  • It never copies page-level restrictions from a blueprint, so a new space cannot inherit invisible

access rules nobody reviewed.

  • It never modifies an existing space other than the ones it provisioned, and then only the blueprint

reference, review date and labels it owns.

  • Every provisioning step is idempotent. A retried or duplicated run does not create a second space.
  • Every destructive-looking action in the interface (bulk approve, delete all app data, archive a

blueprint) requires a confirmation that states exactly what will happen.

Data and network

The app runs entirely on Atlassian's platform. It makes no external network calls of any kind: no analytics, no error reporting, no content delivery network, no remote fonts. The interface is bundled and served from the app itself. There are no credentials, API tokens or secrets in the app.

Full detail is in the Data Security and Privacy Statement and the Privacy Policy linked from the app's Marketplace listing.
